## Reading the result

Depth-based scaling wins on both sides of the trade at once: burst latency drops
~38% at p95 (max turnaround 13.8 s → 8.2 s) because the scaler surges to 6
workers within ~2.5 s of a burst landing, and total worker occupancy drops 36%
because it idles at 1 worker between bursts instead of 3. The 1.5 s modeled
systemd-start penalty is already inside these numbers. The caveats are the
simulation boundary: real researcher tasks are minutes-long LLM calls, not
0.5–1.5 s sleeps, so per-task scaling gains shrink as service time grows relative
to burst spacing; and real queue depth must come from the prompt-engine, whose
current health is questionable (FLEET_SERVICE_ATLAS.md §6 lists
`lumen-prompt-engine` as not-found in the healer fallback table). A production
build should (a) measure real unit-start latency, (b) pick thresholds from the
real queue's burst profile, (c) confirm which queue/depth signal is
authoritative. None of these threaten the direction of the effect — the pattern
(scale to ceil(depth/k) with hysteresis and a floor of 1) is cheap, safe
(no mid-task kills), and beat a conservative bar on the first pre-committed try.
